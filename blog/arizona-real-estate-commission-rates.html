Arizona Real Estate Commission Rates (2026): What Buyers and Sellers Actually Pay

Updated September 2026 • 8 min read

Short answer: the average total real estate commission in Arizona is about 5.71% of the sale price. That is usually split between the listing agent (about 2.88%) and the buyer's agent (about 2.83%). Whether you call it real estate commission, realtor commission, or realtor fees, it is the same thing: what agents are paid when a home sells in AZ.

Key point:
There is no standard or legally required commission in Arizona. Every rate is negotiable, and since August 2024 the rules around who pays the buyer's agent have changed. Here is what that means for you, whether you are buying or selling.

Average Real Estate Commission Rate in Arizona

Based on Clever Real Estate's August 2026 survey of agents, here is how Arizona commissions typically break down:

  • Total commission: about 5.71% of the sale price
  • Listing agent (seller's side): about 2.88%
  • Buyer's agent: about 2.83%

That puts Arizona right around the national average of about 5.70%. Rates in the Phoenix metro area, including Scottsdale, Mesa, Chandler, and Gilbert, generally track the statewide average. Individual agents and brokerages may charge more or less.

Who Pays the Commission After the NAR Settlement?

Traditionally, the seller paid the full commission and it was split between both agents. Changes that took effect on August 17, 2024, following the National Association of Realtors settlement, changed how that works:

  • Buyers must sign a written agreement with their agent before touring a home. The agreement states how much the agent will be paid.
  • Offers of buyer agent pay no longer appear on the MLS. Sellers can still offer it, but it is handled through negotiation instead.
  • Sellers can still pay the buyer's agent. Many do, because it makes the home easier to sell. It is often negotiated as part of the offer.
  • If the seller does not pay, the buyer is responsible for what their written agreement says, which is why it matters to read it carefully.

In practice, many Arizona sellers still cover the buyer's agent. The difference is that it is now openly negotiated rather than assumed.

Is Real Estate Commission Negotiable in Arizona?

Yes, always. No Arizona law sets commission rates. Each brokerage sets its own policies, and each client can negotiate. Sellers can negotiate the listing fee, and buyers can negotiate the terms of their buyer agreement, including whether part of the commission comes back to them.

Real Estate Commission on New Construction Homes

New construction works differently. There is no listing agent. Instead, most production builders in Arizona set aside a budget for buyer agent compensation as part of their marketing costs, and that compensation is often higher than on a typical resale home.

  • The builder pays your agent, not you. It is already built into the builder's sales budget.
  • The builder's sales rep works for the builder. Having your own agent costs you nothing extra.
  • Register your agent on your first visit. Most builders will not pay an agent who was not with you from the start.

Because builder compensation tends to be higher, new construction is where the largest buyer rebates are most common. What Arizona Sellers Should Know

If you are selling, you will typically pay the listing agent's commission, around 2.88% on average. You will also decide whether to offer compensation to the buyer's agent. Offering it can attract more buyers, especially first time buyers who may not have extra cash to pay their own agent. Talk through the tradeoffs with your listing agent before you list.

How Buyers Can Get Part of the Commission Back

Here is the part most buyers never hear about: in Arizona, your buyer's agent can legally rebate part of their commission to you at closing. The rebate shows up as a credit on your settlement statement, so it lowers the cash you need to close.

With RebateAZ, buyers can receive 1% to 2% of the purchase price back at closing, depending on the commission structure. On a $450,000 home, that is $4,500 to $9,000. Rebates toward the higher end are most common on new construction, where builder compensation is higher. You can use it to:

  • Lower your closing costs
  • Buy down your interest rate
  • Reduce the cash you need at closing

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average real estate commission in Arizona?

About 5.71% of the sale price in total, according to Clever Real Estate's August 2026 survey: roughly 2.88% to the listing agent and 2.83% to the buyer's agent.

Does the seller still pay the buyer's agent?

Often, yes. Sellers can still agree to pay the buyer's agent. It is just negotiated directly now instead of being posted on the MLS.

Can I negotiate my agent's commission?

Yes. Commissions in Arizona are always negotiable, on both the buying and selling side.

Can a buyer get part of the commission back?

Yes. Commission rebates are legal in Arizona when disclosed and handled through escrow. See How Commission Rebates Work in Arizona.

Do builders pay buyer agent commission on new construction?

Usually, yes. Most Arizona production builders budget for buyer agent compensation, and it is often higher than on resale homes. Just make sure your agent is registered with the builder on your first visit.
