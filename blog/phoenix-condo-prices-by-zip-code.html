Phoenix Condo Prices Are Falling Faster Than Houses (2026, by Zip Code)

Updated November 2026 • County sales through August 2026

Phoenix-area condo and townhome prices fell about 5.7% per square foot in the 12 months through August 2026, versus 1.8% for single-family homes. Condo prices fell in 41 of the 49 zip codes with enough sales, led by 85226 (Chandler, -11.4%), 85282 (Tempe, -10.3%) and 85257 (Scottsdale, -10.2%). Measured against their own 2022 peak, condos are about 12% lower, while houses are about 5% lower. The median condo sold for $315,000, down from $330,000 a year earlier.

Are condo prices dropping in Phoenix?

Yes. Across the Phoenix area, the median price per square foot of condo and townhome resales fell 5.7% from September 2025 through August 2026 compared with the year before. Single-family homes fell 1.8%. Condos had nearly recovered to their 2022 high by early 2024, then slid; they are now about 12% below that peak.

  • Falling: 41 of 49 zip codes saw condo prices per square foot drop 1% or more.
  • About flat (within 1%): 4 zip codes.
  • Rising: 4 zip codes, all in central Phoenix and the Scottsdale and Paradise Valley area.

Where are condo prices falling the most in Phoenix?

Among zip codes with at least 40 condo resales in each year, the biggest drops in price per square foot were in 85226 (Chandler, -11.4%), 85282 (Tempe, -10.3%), 85257 (Scottsdale, -10.2%), 85202 (Mesa, -9.3%), 85018 (Phoenix, -8.7%), 85201 (Mesa, -8.7%). The steepest drops cluster in older East Valley condo areas: west Mesa, Tempe, south Scottsdale and west Chandler.

Who Buys Condos in Phoenix?

Condo buyers look very different from house buyers. Last year 38% of condo buyers paid cash (houses: 21%), 11% bought through a company such as an LLC (houses: 9%), and only 6% used an FHA loan (houses: 15%). About 3% used a VA loan. If you are financing, expect to compete with cash in retirement and resort areas, and to have more leverage where cash buyers are scarce.

Is it a good time to buy a condo in Phoenix?

The data cannot tell you where prices go next. What it does show is that condo sellers have given up more ground than house sellers, which usually means more room to negotiate price, repairs or seller credits. Before you buy, read the HOA budget and reserve study, check what the dues cover, and confirm your loan works for that complex.

More Questions

Why are condo prices falling faster than houses?

The sales records show what happened, not why. A few things set condos apart in the data: 38% of condo buyers paid cash, versus 21% of house buyers, and only 6% used FHA loans, versus 15% for houses. Fewer loan options and a bigger role for cash and investor buyers make condo prices more sensitive when those buyers pull back. Rising HOA dues and insurance costs are also common concerns for condo buyers, so check them for any complex you are considering.

Can I use an FHA loan to buy a condo in Arizona?

Yes, but the complex usually needs to be on the FHA approved condominium list, and some individual units can get a single-unit approval. Only 6% of Phoenix-area condo sales used FHA last year, so check approval before you fall for a unit. VA loans have a similar approval list.

Where does this data come from?

Every number comes from Maricopa County Recorder affidavits of property value for condo and townhome sales of $100,000 to $10 million, September 2025 through August 2026, compared with the 12 months before. Price change is the median price per square foot of resales: sales by home builders and condo developers (including companies selling six or more units in one zip code) are left out, so new projects and conversions do not skew it. Square footage comes from Maricopa County Assessor records. Rankings use zip codes with at least 40 condo resales in each year.
