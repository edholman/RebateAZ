What If the Seller Won't Pay My Buyer's Agent in Arizona?

Updated November 2026 • 5 min read

Short answer: most Arizona sellers and builders still offer to pay the buyer's agent, but when one does not, you can ask for it in your offer, ask for a lower price instead, or pay the fee yourself. Your agent should tell you which homes offer compensation before you tour them, so there are no surprises.

Why This Happens

Since August 2024, buyers sign a written agreement with their agent that sets the agent's fee, and offers of buyer agent compensation no longer appear on the MLS. Most sellers still offer it to attract buyers, but some list without it. See Arizona commission rates and who pays.

  • Ask in your offer. Many sellers agree, especially when the home has been on the market a while.
  • Ask for a lower price instead. If the seller will not pay your agent, a price cut can leave you in a similar place.
  • Pay the fee yourself. Ask your lender early how your loan treats a fee you pay to your agent.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do most Arizona sellers still pay the buyer's agent?

Most still offer it, and builders almost always do, but it is no longer automatic. Your agent can find out before you tour.

How do I know if a seller offers buyer agent compensation?

Your agent asks the listing agent or checks the listing's broker information before showing you the home.

Is a home without agent compensation a bad deal?

Not necessarily. Factor the fee into your offer price. If the numbers work, it can still be the right home.
