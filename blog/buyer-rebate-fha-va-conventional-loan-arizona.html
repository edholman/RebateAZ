Using a Buyer Rebate With an FHA, VA, or Conventional Loan in Arizona (2026)

Loan rules checked November 2026 • 9 min read

Short answer: yes, you can use a buyer commission rebate with an FHA, VA, or conventional loan in Arizona. The rebate shows up as a credit on your closing statement and pays for closing costs, prepaid taxes and insurance, or points to lower your rate. What it cannot do on a financed purchase is pay your down payment, and each loan type caps how much credit you can receive.

What you pay with RebateAZ:
Your agent keeps 1.5% of the purchase price (minimum $4,500). Everything the seller or builder offers your agent above that comes back to you as a credit at closing, up to 2% of the price. If the seller offers nothing, you owe only that same minimum, and your agent talks it through with you before writing any offer.

How Your Rebate Is Figured

Most Arizona sellers and builders still offer the buyer's agent a commission, usually 2.5% to 3.5% of the price. With RebateAZ the math is simple:

Your rebate = what the seller offers your agent − 1.5% of the price (minimum $4,500)

So on a $400,000 home where the seller offers 3.5%, your agent is paid $14,000, keeps $6,000, and $8,000 comes back to you. You get the same full service either way: showings, negotiation, contract, inspections, and closing. This is how a rebate differs from a flat-fee agent. Instead of paying a fee out of pocket, you keep most of the commission the seller is already paying.

Credit Limits by Loan Type

Lenders treat a credit from your agent as an interested party contribution, the same bucket as credits from the seller or builder. Every loan program caps the total of those credits, and the credits can only pay real costs on your closing statement.

The good news: a rebate of 1.5% to 2% almost always fits under these caps by itself. Problems only start when the rebate is stacked on top of a large seller credit, which is covered below.

Four Arizona Examples

1. FHA resale in Buckeye: $310,000

  • Seller offers the buyer's agent 3%: $9,300
  • Agent keeps 1.5%: $4,650
  • Your rebate: $4,650
  • FHA cap: 6% of $310,000 = $18,600, so the rebate fits easily
  • It covers closing costs and prepaids. It cannot cover the 3.5% down payment ($10,850).

2. Conventional with 5% down in Mesa: $450,000

  • Seller offers 3%: $13,500
  • Agent keeps 1.5%: $6,750
  • Your rebate: $6,750
  • Conventional cap with under 10% down: 3% = $13,500, so there is room for a seller credit of up to $6,750 on top

3. New build in Surprise or Queen Creek: $400,000

  • Builder offers 3.5%: $14,000
  • Agent keeps 1.5%: $6,000
  • Your rebate: $8,000, the full 2%
  • Builders often add their own closing cost incentive. Your lender adds the builder credit and the rebate together against the cap, so we plan both before you sign.

4. VA purchase in Peoria: $350,000

  • Seller offers 2.5%: $8,750
  • Agent keeps 1.5%: $5,250
  • Your rebate: $3,500
  • Applied to closing costs or the VA funding fee, within your lender's VA concession rules

Examples are estimates. Your rebate depends on the commission offered on the home you buy and your loan's rules.

The Mistake That Wastes a Rebate

Because the rebate can only pay real closing costs, it competes with seller credits for the same space. If you ask the seller to pay all of your closing costs and you have a rebate coming, the total can be more than your costs or more than your loan's cap. The extra does not come back to you as cash. It is cut from the credit or used to lower the price the lender uses.

The fix is to plan the offer around the rebate:

  • Ask for a lower price instead of a large seller credit when the rebate already covers most of your closing costs.
  • Put the extra toward points to buy down your interest rate, if your loan allows it.
  • Get a closing cost estimate from your lender before you write the offer, so you know how much room you have.

Rebates With Down Payment Assistance

Arizona assistance programs like HOME Plus and Home in Five pay toward your down payment, and a rebate can then cover closing costs. Assistance programs do not allow cash back at closing, so the rebate is applied to allowable costs. See our guide to Arizona first time home buyer programs for current income limits and how much help each program gives.

How to Set It Up

  1. Sign a buyer agreement that spells out the rebate. Arizona allows rebates when they are disclosed, and the agreement sets how your agent is paid.
  2. Tell your lender about the rebate at preapproval. They will confirm how much credit your loan allows.
  3. Write the credit into your purchase contract. Your agent handles the wording.
  4. Check your Closing Disclosure. The rebate appears as a credit to you from your agent's brokerage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a buyer rebate with an FHA loan?

Yes. FHA allows credits from interested parties, including your agent, up to 6% of the sales price combined. The rebate can pay closing costs and prepaids but not your 3.5% minimum down payment.

Can a rebate pay my down payment?

Not on a financed purchase. Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, and FHA all bar credits from interested parties from being used for the down payment. Down payment money has to come from you, a gift, or an approved assistance program.

Can I get the rebate as cash at closing?

Usually not when you use a loan, because the credit is limited to your actual closing costs. Cash buyers can receive it as a credit on the settlement statement.

What is your total buyer representation fee?

1.5% of the purchase price, with a $4,500 minimum. In most deals the seller or builder pays it as part of the commission they already offer, and anything above that comes back to you.

Do you offer flat-fee or reduced-commission buyer representation?

We offer a rebate, which works out better for most buyers. You get full service, the seller's offered commission pays your agent, and everything above 1.5% (minimum $4,500) is credited back to you, up to 2% of the price.

What if the seller is already paying my closing costs?

Then plan the offer so the two credits do not overlap. Asking for a lower price, or using the rebate for points, keeps you from losing part of it to your loan's caps.

Does the rebate work on new construction?

Yes, and new builds often pay the largest rebates because builders commonly offer higher agent commissions. You must bring your agent, or register them, on your first visit to the builder. See rebates on new construction in Arizona.
