Buyer Rebate vs. Flat-Fee Agent vs. Lender Credit: Ways to Save on a Buyer's Agent in Arizona (2026)

Updated November 2026 • 8 min read

Short answer: Arizona buyers have four main ways to keep part of the buyer's agent commission: a rebate brokerage, a flat-fee buyer service, a lender-tied credit from a big brokerage, or a traditional agent who keeps the full commission. The right choice depends on how much help you want, which lender you want to use, and how much credit your loan can actually absorb.

The one thing most comparisons miss:
On a financed purchase, a credit can only pay your real closing costs, and every loan caps total credits. A bigger promised refund does not help if your loan cannot use it. See credit limits by loan type.

The Four Options

1. Rebate brokerage (like RebateAZ)

  • How it works: a full-service licensed agent represents you, and part of the commission the seller or builder pays comes back to you as a credit at closing.
  • RebateAZ terms: your agent keeps 1.5% of the price (minimum $4,500). Everything above that comes back to you, up to 2% of the price.
  • Lender: use any lender you want.
  • Service: as many showings as you need, comps and offer strategy, negotiation, inspections and repair requests, and closing, with no per-showing or upfront fees.
  • Homes: resale and new construction.

2. Flat-fee buyer service

  • How it works: you pay a set fee, and the rest of the commission comes back to you. For example, ShopProp advertises a $1,995 flat fee in Phoenix on its website.
  • Trade-off: the dollar amount back can be larger, but you usually get less help. Many flat-fee services limit how many homes they show, charge extra for showings after the first or for added services, or collect part of the fee up front. Ask for the full fee schedule in writing.
  • You may be doing more yourself: finding homes, pulling comps, judging inspection reports, and pushing repair requests are often left to you or cost extra.
  • Watch for: refunds larger than your closing costs or your loan's credit cap. The extra is cut at closing, not paid to you.

3. Lender-tied credit from a large brokerage

  • How it works: you get a credit only if you also use the brokerage's affiliated lender.
  • Example: Redfin's website describes a lender credit of 0.75% of the loan amount, up to $6,000, for buyers who use a Redfin agent and finance with Rocket Mortgage, in select markets. Larger amounts are advertised for buying and selling together.
  • Trade-off: the savings depend on using that one lender, so compare its rate and fees with other lenders before you count the credit.

4. Traditional agent

  • How it works: the agent keeps the full commission the seller or builder offers. No money back.
  • When it makes sense: if you already have an agent you trust and the relationship matters more to you than the savings.

What Full Service Includes

The money back only tells half the story. Buying a home in the Phoenix area usually means touring several homes, sometimes over weeks, and the agent's work after the offer is accepted is where buyers save or lose the most. With RebateAZ, the rebate comes with:

  • Showings: as many homes as you need to see, with no per-showing fees.
  • Research: comparable sales, property records, and HOA and permit details pulled for homes you are serious about.
  • Offer strategy and negotiation: price, terms, and how to fit the rebate and any seller credit under your loan's limits.
  • Inspections and repairs: your agent attends, reviews the report with you, and negotiates the repair request.
  • Through closing: coordination with your lender and title company, and the rebate confirmed on your Closing Disclosure.
  • No upfront fees. Your agent is paid at closing from the commission the seller or builder offers.

Questions to Ask Any of Them

  1. Exactly how much comes back to me on this home, in dollars? Get it in writing in your buyer agreement.
  2. Do I have to use your lender, title company, or another service to get it?
  3. Who shows me homes, writes the offer, negotiates repairs, and attends inspections?
  4. Is there a limit on showings, a charge per showing, or any fee I pay up front or if I do not buy? Get the full fee schedule in writing.
  5. What happens if the seller offers no commission? At RebateAZ, the minimum is $4,500 or 1.5% of the price, whichever is higher, and your agent discusses it with you before writing any offer.
  6. Does it work on new construction? Some Arizona programs only do new builds, others only resale.
  7. Will my loan allow the full credit? Ask your lender before you write an offer.

Which One Fits You

  • You want full service and real money back, with any lender: a rebate brokerage.
  • You are experienced, comfortable doing more yourself, and paying cash or putting a lot down: a flat-fee service can return more, as long as you are fine with limited showings and possible extra fees.
  • You already planned to use that brokerage's lender and its rate is competitive: a lender-tied credit is easy money.
  • You have an agent you love: stay with them, and ask whether they offer any credit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a buyer rebate and a flat-fee buyer agent?

With a rebate, a full-service agent keeps part of the commission and credits you the rest at closing. With a flat-fee service, you pay a set fee and get back what is left, but services are often limited, and some charge per showing, for added services, or up front.

Is a bigger refund always better?

Not on a financed purchase. A credit can only pay your actual closing costs and must fit under your loan's cap on credits. Anything above that is cut at closing.

Do I have to use a specific lender to get a rebate from RebateAZ?

No. You can use any lender. Lender-tied programs from some large brokerages require their affiliated lender to get the credit.

Are buyer rebates legal in Arizona?

Yes, when they are disclosed, written into the buyer agreement, and shown on the closing statement. See Is a Realtor Rebate Legal in Arizona?

How much will I get back with RebateAZ?

The commission offered to your agent minus 1.5% of the price (minimum $4,500), up to 2% of the price. On a $550,000 home with a 3% commission, that is $8,250. See examples at $500,000 and $700,000 to $1 million.
