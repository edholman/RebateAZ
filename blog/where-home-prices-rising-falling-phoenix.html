Where Are Home Prices Rising and Falling in Phoenix? (2026, by Zip Code)

By Esteban Holman, HomeSmart • Updated November 2026 • County sales September 2025 to August 2026

In the 12 months through August 2026, home prices rose the most in 85018 (Phoenix, +6.1%), 85260 (Scottsdale, +6.1%) and 85262 (Scottsdale, +5.4%), and fell the most in 85017 (Phoenix, -12.3%), 85009 (Phoenix, -9.5%) and 85035 (Phoenix, -9.3%). Of 123 Phoenix-area zip codes, 12 saw home prices per square foot rise at least 1%, 95 fell at least 1%, and 16 were about flat. Overall, prices per square foot fell 1.8% across the metro, September 2025 through August 2026 versus the year before. Every price change on this page compares the median price per square foot of single-family home resales, so condos, new builds and a shift toward bigger or smaller homes do not skew it.

Which areas of Phoenix are appreciating?

Among zip codes with at least 100 single-family resales, prices rose the most in 85018 (Phoenix, +6.1%), 85260 (Scottsdale, +6.1%), 85262 (Scottsdale, +5.4%), 85253 (Paradise Valley, +4.0%), 85007 (Phoenix, +3.7%), 85259 (Scottsdale, +2.4%). Gains were modest and concentrated in Arcadia, downtown Phoenix, Paradise Valley and north Scottsdale. In the East Valley, only two Mesa zip codes and one Tempe zip code edged up.

Where are home prices dropping in Phoenix?

The biggest declines among zip codes with at least 100 single-family resales were in 85017 (Phoenix, -12.3%), 85009 (Phoenix, -9.5%), 85035 (Phoenix, -9.3%), 85021 (Phoenix, -8.8%), 85013 (Phoenix, -8.3%), 85033 (Phoenix, -7.9%). Declines were widespread but mostly small: 95 of 123 zip codes fell at least 1%, most of them by less than 5%. The steepest drops were in west and central Phoenix.

Is the Phoenix housing market going down?

Prices softened but did not crash. Price per square foot on single-family resales changed -1.8% year over year across the metro. 95 zip codes fell at least 1%, but 78 of those drops were under 5%, and 12 zip codes still rose. Higher-priced areas like Scottsdale held up best, while many entry-level and mid-priced areas dipped. This looks back at recorded sales and is not a forecast.

  • Appreciating: 12 zip codes, mostly north Scottsdale, Paradise Valley, Arcadia and downtown Phoenix, plus two Mesa zip codes and one in Tempe.
  • Declining: 95 zip codes, including all of Chandler, Peoria and Surprise and nearly all of Mesa, Gilbert, Glendale and North Phoenix.
  • About flat (within 1%): 16 zip codes.

What are the best zip codes to buy in Phoenix right now?

There is no single best zip code, but the data shows where buyers have the most leverage: zip codes where price per square foot fell at least 2% and fewer buyers paid cash or bought through a company than the metro average (23% cash, 9% company). In the East Valley and North Phoenix that includes 85087 (New River), 85083 (Phoenix), 85249 (Chandler), 85029 (Phoenix), 85212 (Mesa), 85027 (Phoenix). Falling prices with less cash competition usually mean more room to negotiate price, repairs or seller credits. It does not mean prices will rise later.

What This Means for Your Offer

In a zip code where prices are declining, sellers are more likely to take a lower price, pay for repairs, or give a credit toward your closing costs or a rate buydown. In a zip code that is appreciating, you may need a cleaner offer and less room to ask for credits. Either way, a buyer rebate puts cash back in your pocket at closing: on a $475,000 home, that is about $7,120 when the seller offers 3%.

Questions

Where does this data come from?

Maricopa County Recorder affidavits of property value for single family homes, condos and townhomes sold for $100,000 to $10 million, September 2025 through August 2026, compared with the prior 12 months. Price change compares the median price per square foot of single-family home resales. Condos, townhomes and homes sold by builders are left out, and price per square foot adjusts for home size, because a shift in what sold, like a wave of new construction, more condo sales or more big homes, can move a zip code median by 10% or more on its own. Square footage comes from Maricopa County Assessor records. Zip codes need at least 30 sales to appear, and rankings use zip codes with at least 100 single-family resales. The median still reflects which homes resold, so it is not what any one home gained or lost.

Sources: Maricopa County Recorder affidavits of property value (single family, condo and townhouse sales, $100,000 to $10 million, multi-parcel sales excluded), September 2024 to August 2026. Price change is the median price per square foot of single-family home resales (condos, townhomes and sales by home builders left out; square footage from Maricopa County Assessor records).
