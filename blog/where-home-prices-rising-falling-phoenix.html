Where Are Home Prices Rising and Falling in Phoenix? (2026, by Zip Code)

Updated November 2026 • County sales September 2025 to August 2026

In the 12 months through August 2026, home prices rose the most in 85018 (Phoenix, +20.4%), 85003 (Phoenix, +12.8%) and 85258 (Scottsdale, +11.6%), and fell the most in 85012 (Phoenix, -12.2%), 85390 (Wickenburg, -10.6%) and 85009 (Phoenix, -10.0%). Of 124 Phoenix-area zip codes, 26 saw the median resale price rise at least 1%, 82 fell at least 1%, and 16 were about flat. Overall, the metro median resale price was $467,000 from September 2025 through August 2026, versus $472,500 the year before (-1.2%). New builds are left out of every price change on this page so builder sales do not skew it.

Where are home prices dropping in Phoenix?

The biggest declines among zip codes with at least 100 resales were in 85012 (Phoenix, -12.2%), 85390 (Wickenburg, -10.6%), 85009 (Phoenix, -10.0%), 85017 (Phoenix, -9.6%), 85054 (Phoenix, -9.6%), 85210 (Mesa, -7.9%). Declines were widespread but mostly small: 82 of 124 zip codes fell at least 1%. Every Chandler zip code and all but one in Mesa were down, and almost all of North Phoenix slipped.

Is the Phoenix housing market going down?

Prices softened but did not crash. The metro median resale price changed -1.2% year over year, from $472,500 to $467,000. 82 zip codes fell at least 1%, but 60 of those drops were under 5%, and 26 zip codes still rose. Higher-priced areas like Scottsdale held up best, while many entry-level and mid-priced areas dipped. This looks back at recorded sales and is not a forecast.

  • Appreciating: 26 zip codes, mostly Scottsdale, Fountain Hills, Cave Creek, Gilbert and parts of central Phoenix.
  • Declining: 82 zip codes, including all of Chandler, nearly all of Mesa and North Phoenix, and most of Glendale, Peoria and Surprise.
  • About flat (within 1%): 16 zip codes.

What are the best zip codes to buy in Phoenix right now?

There is no single best zip code, but the data shows where buyers have the most leverage: zip codes where the resale median fell at least 2% and fewer buyers paid cash or bought through a company than the metro average (23% cash, 9% company). In the East Valley and North Phoenix that includes 85286 (Chandler), 85087 (New River), 85023 (Phoenix), 85029 (Phoenix), 85233 (Gilbert), 85083 (Phoenix). Falling prices with less cash competition usually mean more room to negotiate price, repairs or seller credits. It does not mean prices will rise later.

What This Means for Your Offer

In a zip code where prices are declining, sellers are more likely to take a lower price, pay for repairs, or give a credit toward your closing costs or a rate buydown. In a zip code that is appreciating, you may need a cleaner offer and less room to ask for credits. Either way, a buyer rebate puts cash back in your pocket at closing: on a $475,000 home, that is about $7,120 when the seller offers 3%.

Questions

Where does this data come from?

Maricopa County Recorder affidavits of property value for single family homes, condos and townhomes sold for $100,000 to $10 million, September 2025 through August 2026, compared with the prior 12 months. Price change compares resales only: homes sold by builders are left out, because a wave of new construction can move a zip code median by 10% or more on its own. Zip codes need at least 30 sales to appear, and rankings use zip codes with at least 100 resales. The median still reflects which homes resold, so it is not what any one home gained or lost.

Sources: Maricopa County Recorder affidavits of property value (single family, condo and townhouse sales, $100,000 to $10 million, multi-parcel sales excluded), September 2024 to August 2026. Price change uses resales only (sales by home builders left out). See how we research our numbers.
