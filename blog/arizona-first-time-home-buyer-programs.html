Arizona First Time Home Buyer Programs (2026): Down Payment Assistance, Grants, and Ways to Save

Program details verified September 2026 • 12 min read

Short answer: Arizona has several strong down payment assistance programs in 2026, and some of them do not require you to be a first time buyer. Depending on where you buy and your income, you may qualify for help worth 3% to 10% of your purchase, plus low down payment loans like FHA, VA, and USDA.

The big picture:
Down payment assistance helps with the money you need up front. A buyer rebate from RebateAZ can help with the rest by lowering your closing costs. Used together, they can dramatically cut the cash you need to buy your first home in Arizona.

Statewide: HOME Plus

HOME Plus (Arizona Industrial Development Authority)

The most widely used program in Arizona, available in every county with no first time buyer requirement.

  • Assistance: up to 4% of your first mortgage, as a 0% second loan with no payments
  • Forgiveness: forgiven in full after 5 years. Sell or refinance before then and you repay it.
  • Income limit: $155,386 in all counties (effective April 6, 2026), based on the borrowers on the loan
  • Price limit: none beyond normal loan limits
  • Minimum credit score: 620
  • Loan types: FHA, VA, USDA, and conventional (Fannie Mae HFA Preferred, Freddie Mac HFA Advantage)
  • Education: one borrower must complete a homebuyer course before closing
  • Funding: available year round

Official HOME Plus site

Phoenix and Maricopa County Programs

Home in Five Advantage (Phoenix IDA and Maricopa County IDA)

Available anywhere in Maricopa County, including Phoenix, Mesa, Chandler, Gilbert, Glendale, Scottsdale, Tempe, Peoria, Surprise, Goodyear, and Buckeye.

  • Assistance: 3% to 6% of your first mortgage, with no dollar cap
  • Options: several repayment versions, including a 3 year forgivable option on FHA and VA loans. Some options have used up their funds for 2026, so ask your lender what is open.
  • First time buyer: not required for most options
  • Income limit: $157,360 (guidelines effective June 10, 2026)
  • Price limit: none
  • Minimum credit score: 640
  • Loan types: FHA, VA, and conventional
  • Education: an 8 hour course from an Arizona HUD approved agency

Official Home in Five Advantage site

City of Phoenix Open Doors

The most generous program in the metro for buyers who qualify, but only within Phoenix city limits.

  • Assistance: up to 10% of the purchase price, based on need
  • Forgiveness: a 0% deferred loan, forgiven after an affordability period of up to 15 years
  • First time buyer: required
  • Income limit: 80% of area median income (effective June 1, 2026): $62,950 for 1 person, $71,950 for 2, $80,950 for 3, $89,900 for 4
  • Price limit: $470,250
  • Other requirements: at least $1,000 of your own money, an 8 hour class plus one on one counseling, a home inspection, and a 2 year home warranty
  • How to apply: through Trellis or Chicanos Por La Causa (CPLC). Funding is limited.

Official City of Phoenix homeownership page

Other cities in the valley offer smaller or specialized programs from time to time. For example, Chandler runs a first time buyer program through a community land trust. If you are buying in a specific city, ask your lender or check the city's housing department for anything currently funded.

Tucson and Pima County Programs

Pima Tucson Homebuyer's Solution

The main assistance program in Pima County, run by the Industrial Development Authorities of Pima County and the City of Tucson.

  • Assistance: a percentage of your loan amount, as a 0% forgivable second loan (a grant on VA loans)
  • First time buyer: not required
  • Income limit: $155,386 for FHA, VA, USDA, and most conventional loans (effective May 21, 2026)
  • Price limit: none beyond normal loan limits
  • Minimum credit score: 640
  • Education: required for all borrowers

Official Pima IDA program page

City of Tucson and Pima County HOME Down Payment Assistance

Larger help for lower income buyers in Tucson and Pima County.

  • Assistance: the lesser of $50,000 or 20% of the contract price, as a deferred loan repaid when you sell
  • Income limit: 80% of area median income: $55,900 for 1 person, $63,900 for 2, $71,900 for 3, $79,850 for 4
  • Price limit: $304,000 for existing homes, $345,000 for new construction
  • Other requirements: at least $1,000 of your own money, 2 months of reserves after closing, HUD certified counseling before you sign a contract

Official City of Tucson program page

What about the Pima County Lighthouse program? PimaTucson LIGHTHOUSE has closed and is not currently offered. Pima County buyers can also look at the Mortgage Credit Certificate, a federal tax credit on part of your mortgage interest. It is scheduled to reopen to new applications on October 1, 2026.

Pinal County and Rural Arizona

Buying in San Tan Valley, Maricopa, Casa Grande, or elsewhere outside Maricopa and Pima counties? You have two extra options:

Arizona Is Home (Arizona IDA)

  • Assistance: 4% of your loan plus a below market interest rate
  • Repayment: 0% with no payments, repaid in full when you sell or refinance
  • First time buyer: required, with exceptions for veterans and targeted areas
  • Where: every Arizona county except Maricopa and Pima (and Chino Valley)
  • Income limit: 100% of area median by county. In Pinal County, $112,400.
  • Price limit: $481,176
  • Minimum credit score: 620
  • Funding: capped at $55 million for 2026 and may run out before year end. Ask your lender if it is still open.

Official Arizona Is Home page

USDA loans are also worth checking in Pinal County and on the edges of the valley. They allow 0% down in eligible areas. Look up any address on the USDA eligibility map.

Low Down Payment Loans for Arizona Buyers

Assistance programs are layered on top of a regular mortgage. These are the most common options for first time buyers:

  • FHA: 3.5% down with a credit score of 580 or higher. The 2026 FHA loan limit for a single family home is $557,750 in Maricopa and Pinal counties and $541,287 in Pima County.
  • VA: 0% down for eligible veterans and service members.
  • USDA: 0% down in eligible rural and suburban areas, with household income limits.
  • Conventional 3% down: Fannie Mae HomeReady and Freddie Mac Home Possible allow 3% down for buyers at or below 80% of area median income.

Stacking a Buyer Rebate With Down Payment Assistance

Down payment assistance covers your down payment. But you still have closing costs, often thousands of dollars on top of the down payment. That is where a buyer rebate helps.

With RebateAZ, part of your agent's commission comes back to you as a credit at closing, typically 1% to 2% of the purchase price depending on the commission structure. On a $400,000 home, that is $4,000 to $8,000 that can go toward:

  • Your closing costs and prepaid items like taxes and insurance
  • Buying down your interest rate
  • Lowering your loan amount, when your program allows it
Know the rules:
Arizona down payment assistance programs do not allow cash back to the buyer at closing. When you use one, your rebate is applied to allowable costs instead of paid out as cash. Loans also cap credits to the buyer (for example, 6% on FHA and 3% to 9% on conventional loans, depending on your down payment). We work with your lender to structure the rebate so you get the full benefit within those rules.

Step by Step: How to Buy Your First Home in Arizona

  1. Check your credit and budget. Most assistance programs need a score of 620 to 640 or higher.
  2. Take a homebuyer education course. Nearly every program requires one, and Home in Five and Open Doors require 8 hours. Starting early saves time.
  3. Get preapproved with a participating lender. Assistance programs only work through approved lenders, so ask up front which programs they offer.
  4. Choose your agent and sign a buyer agreement. Ask about a buyer rebate before you sign, since the agreement sets how your agent is paid.
  5. Visiting new builds? Bring your agent the first time. Builders usually require your agent to register on your first visit.
  6. Make an offer, inspect, and close. Your assistance and rebate both show up on your closing statement.

Frequently Asked Questions

What first time home buyer programs are available in Arizona in 2026?

The main ones are HOME Plus (statewide), Home in Five Advantage (Maricopa County), City of Phoenix Open Doors, Pima Tucson Homebuyer's Solution, the Tucson and Pima County HOME program, and Arizona Is Home (outside Maricopa and Pima). FHA, VA, USDA, and 3% down conventional loans are also available.

Do I have to be a first time buyer to get down payment assistance?

Not always. HOME Plus, Home in Five Advantage, and Pima Tucson Homebuyer's Solution do not require it. Phoenix Open Doors and Arizona Is Home do.

What is the income limit for HOME Plus?

$155,386 in every Arizona county as of April 6, 2026.

Can I use a buyer rebate with down payment assistance?

In many cases, yes. The rebate is applied as a credit toward allowable closing costs, prepaids, or a rate buydown within your loan's limits. It cannot be paid out as cash on assistance programs, and your lender must approve how it is applied.

Is the Pima County Lighthouse program still available?

No. PimaTucson LIGHTHOUSE has closed. The current Pima County program is Pima Tucson Homebuyer's Solution.
