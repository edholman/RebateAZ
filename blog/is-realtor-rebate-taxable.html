Is a Realtor Rebate Taxable? What the IRS Has Said About Buyer Rebates

Updated November 2026 • 4 min read

Short answer: generally no. In a 2007 private letter ruling (PLR 200721013), the IRS concluded that a buyer's agent commission rebate paid or credited to the buyer at closing is an adjustment to the purchase price, not income, and that the broker does not need to report it on a 1099. The trade-off is that it lowers your cost basis in the home. This is general information, not tax advice; confirm with your tax professional.

Why It Is Not Income

The IRS reasoning goes back to a 1977 Tax Court case, Freedom Newspapers, which treated a payment from a broker to a buyer as a reduction of the price paid rather than income. A rebate works the same way: you paid the seller's price, and part of it effectively came back to you.

A private letter ruling is written for the taxpayer who asked for it and cannot be cited as precedent by others, but it shows how the IRS views this type of payment.

What It Means for Your Cost Basis

Your cost basis is generally what you paid for the home plus certain closing costs. A rebate reduces it. For example, on a $450,000 home with a $6,750 rebate, your basis starts from about $443,250 instead of $450,000. For most homeowners this only matters if you sell for a gain above the home sale exclusion, or if the home is a rental and you depreciate it.

What to Keep

  • Your buyer agreement showing the rebate
  • Your Closing Disclosure or settlement statement showing the credit

Frequently Asked Questions

Will I get a 1099 for my buyer rebate?

Generally you should not. The 2007 IRS ruling concluded the broker has no 1099 reporting obligation for a rebate credited to the buyer, because it is not income to the buyer.

Do I report the rebate on my tax return?

Not as income, generally. It reduces the price you paid, which lowers your cost basis in the home. Ask your tax professional how it applies to you.

Is a rebate on a rental property treated the same way?

The same purchase price logic applies, so the rebate lowers the basis you depreciate. Confirm the details with your tax professional.

Sources (checked November 2026): IRS, PLR 200721013 (2007).
