How the Math Works

  1. The seller or builder offers your agent a commission, usually 2.5% to 3.5% in the Phoenix area. Your agent tells you the amount before you tour a home.
  2. Your agent keeps 1.5% of the price, with a $4,500 minimum. You get full service: showings, comps, negotiation, inspections and closing, with no upfront or per-showing fees.
  3. Everything above that comes back to you as a credit at closing, up to 2% of the price.
  4. Your loan sets the ceiling: conventional loans allow 3%, 6% or 9% in total credits depending on your down payment, FHA allows 6%, and the rebate and any seller credit share that limit. See credit limits by loan type.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a buyer rebate on a $500,000 home in Arizona?

With RebateAZ, $7,500 if the seller offers your agent 3%, $5,000 at 2.5%, and $10,000 (the 2% maximum) at 3.5%.

What if the seller offers less than 1.5%?

Then there is no rebate, and the difference up to your agent's 1.5% fee (minimum $4,500) is yours to pay or to ask the seller for in your offer. Your agent tells you before you write an offer. See what to do if the seller won't pay your agent.

Why is the rebate capped at 2%?

The 2% cap is part of the RebateAZ program terms. It also keeps the rebate in the range a financed buyer can actually use, since a credit can only pay real closing costs, prepaid items and points.

Can my loan use the whole rebate?

Usually. A 2% rebate fits under every conventional and FHA limit by itself. Problems start only when it is stacked on a large seller credit, or when it is more than your actual closing costs. The calculator above checks the loan limit for you.
